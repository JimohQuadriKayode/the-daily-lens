Infection Prevention Starts With You: Protect Your Home, School, Workplace and Community

Simple infection prevention and control practices can help protect individuals, families and communities from infectious diseases.

Published by The Daily Lens • Health & Community

Why Infection Prevention Matters

Infectious diseases can spread quickly when people live, study, work or gather closely together. While healthcare workers and public-health authorities play an important role in preventing and controlling outbreaks, individuals and communities also have an important part to play.

Good Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) practices can help reduce the spread of infections and protect families, schools, workplaces and communities.

How Can We Reduce the Spread of Infections?

Everyone has a role to play in breaking the chain of transmission. Simple everyday habits can make a significant difference.

🧼 1. Wash Your Hands Regularly

Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and clean running water, especially before eating or preparing food, after using the toilet and after contact with potentially contaminated materials.

🤧 2. Practise Good Respiratory Hygiene

Cover your mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing. Dispose of used tissues properly and clean your hands afterwards.

👥 3. Avoid Close Contact When You Are Sick

If you develop symptoms of an infectious illness, avoid unnecessarily exposing other people and seek appropriate medical advice, particularly if symptoms are severe or getting worse.

🧽 4. Keep Frequently Touched Surfaces Clean

Regular cleaning of frequently touched surfaces can help reduce contamination in homes, schools, offices and other shared environments.

🥤 5. Avoid Sharing Personal Items

Where appropriate, avoid sharing personal-use items that may come into contact with bodily fluids, such as towels and similar items.

🏫 6. Make Schools and Workplaces Part of the Solution

Schools, offices and other shared environments should encourage good hand hygiene, cleanliness and early reporting of illness.

Clean hands save lives.

Infection Prevention Is Everyone's Responsibility

Healthcare workers cannot prevent the spread of infectious diseases alone. Families, teachers, students, workers, religious communities, business owners and community leaders all have a role to play.

Making good hygiene a daily habit can help create healthier and safer communities.

When You Are Unwell

Do not ignore serious or persistent symptoms. Seek appropriate medical attention rather than relying on self-medication.

Public health reminder: For current disease alerts and official public-health guidance in Nigeria, follow information from the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) and qualified healthcare professionals.

Conclusion

Preventing infectious diseases does not belong to healthcare workers alone. It begins with each of us.

By washing our hands regularly, practising good hygiene, keeping shared environments clean, avoiding unnecessary exposure when we are sick and following advice from health authorities, we can help protect ourselves and the people around us.

Prevention is everyone's responsibility.
