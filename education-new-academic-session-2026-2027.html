A New Academic Session, A New Opportunity: How We Can Make 2026/2027 a Successful School Year

As students prepare to begin a new academic session in Jigawa State, success will depend on more than textbooks and examinations. Parents, students, teachers, schools and communities all have a role to play.

Published by The Daily Lens • Education & Community
A new academic session is an opportunity for students, families, teachers and schools to begin again with clear goals and renewed commitment.

A New Beginning for Students and Families

A new academic session is more than a return to the classroom. It is a fresh opportunity for students to improve, for parents to become more involved, for teachers to support their learners and for schools and communities to create conditions in which children can succeed.

As the 2026/2027 academic session gets underway, the focus should not be only on examination results. Education is also about discipline, confidence, curiosity, good character, practical skills and the ability to solve problems.

Jigawa's education investment

Jigawa State has recently announced major investments in educational infrastructure. The State Executive Council approved more than ₦517 million for renovation and construction at selected Integrated Education Board schools and more than ₦12.68 billion for construction, supply and renovation work across over 50 senior secondary schools.

Such investments can improve the physical learning environment, but maintaining good schools is a shared responsibility involving school authorities, students, parents and communities.

1. Parents: Make the Home a Place for Learning

Education does not stop when a child leaves the school gate. The home has a major influence on how children approach learning.

Parents can help by establishing regular sleeping and study routines, checking school attendance, encouraging reading and asking children about what they learned during the day.

Parents should also maintain respectful communication with teachers and school administrators when concerns arise. Early communication can prevent small academic or behavioural problems from becoming bigger ones.

Parents can support learning by creating a regular study routine and taking an interest in their children's education.

2. Students: Start the Session With Purpose

Students should begin the new session with clear and realistic goals. Good performance usually comes from consistent effort, not from waiting until examinations are close.

Attend school regularly, arrive on time, listen carefully, take useful notes, complete assignments and ask questions when something is not understood.

Students should also learn to manage distractions. Phones, social media, entertainment and other activities can have a place in life, but they should not replace study, sleep and responsibilities.

A simple student checklist:

✓ Attend school regularly.

✓ Read and review lessons every day.

✓ Complete assignments on time.

✓ Ask questions when you do not understand.

✓ Respect teachers and classmates.

✓ Avoid unnecessary distractions.

3. Teachers: Identify Difficulties Early

Teachers have an important role in helping students who are struggling academically, socially or emotionally.

A student who becomes unusually quiet, repeatedly misses assignments or falls behind in class may need additional attention and encouragement.

Teachers can help by creating classrooms where students feel comfortable asking questions, making mistakes and trying again. Constructive feedback can help students understand what they need to improve without discouraging them.

Teachers can help students succeed by encouraging participation, answering questions and identifying learning difficulties early.

4. Schools: Create Safe and Supportive Learning Environments

A good school is more than a building. Students need clean, safe and supportive environments where they can concentrate on learning.

School management should pay attention to classroom conditions, sanitation, attendance, discipline and communication with parents.

Schools should also encourage students to report problems early and create an environment where every child is treated with dignity and respect.

5. Communities: Education Is Everyone's Responsibility

Schools do not exist separately from the communities around them. Parents, community leaders, religious leaders, youth groups and other residents can all contribute to creating an environment that values education.

Communities can help protect school facilities, encourage regular attendance and discourage behaviour that keeps children away from school.

When communities see schools as shared institutions rather than places that belong only to government or teachers, everyone has a stronger reason to protect and improve them.

Strong communities can support schools by protecting facilities, encouraging attendance and valuing education.

6. Do Not Measure Every Child by the Same Standard

Every child learns differently. One student may perform strongly in Mathematics while another may excel in writing, communication, technology, agriculture, creative work or practical skills.

Academic achievement is important, but children also need opportunities to develop confidence, discipline, creativity, communication and problem-solving abilities.

Parents and teachers should encourage children to improve while avoiding constant comparisons with other students.

Every child deserves the opportunity to learn, grow and discover what they can contribute to society.

7. The First 30 Days Matter

The beginning of a school year can establish habits that continue throughout the session. Students and families can use the first month to build routines that support long-term success.

A student who develops the habit of attending regularly, reading consistently, completing assignments and asking questions from the beginning is more likely to have a strong foundation for the rest of the academic year.

A 30-day challenge for students:

📚 Read something educational every day.

⏰ Arrive at school on time.

📝 Complete your assignments.

🙋 Ask at least one question when you need clarification.

🔄 Review what you learned before the next lesson.

🤝 Treat teachers and classmates with respect.

A Successful School Year Requires Everyone

The responsibility for education cannot rest on students alone. Parents provide support at home. Teachers provide guidance in the classroom. Schools provide structure and learning environments. Communities help protect and strengthen the institutions around them.

Government investment in classrooms, equipment and other educational facilities is important, but the benefits are greater when the people who use and manage those facilities also help maintain them.

The new academic session should therefore be seen as a shared opportunity: an opportunity to learn more, work together and give children the support they need to reach their potential.

A successful academic year is built when parents provide support, teachers provide guidance, schools provide safe learning environments and communities protect the value of education.

Conclusion

As students begin the 2026/2027 academic session, the goal should be bigger than simply passing examinations. We should aim to develop knowledgeable, responsible, confident and capable young people who can contribute positively to their families and society.

Every parent, student, teacher, school and community has a role to play. If we work together from the beginning of the session, small daily efforts can produce meaningful results by the end of the school year.

A new academic session is a new opportunity. Let us make it count.
