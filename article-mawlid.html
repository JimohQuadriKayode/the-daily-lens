Say No to Maulid An-Nabiyy

A reminder based on the Qur'an, Sunnah, and the understanding of the Salaf.

By Abu Mus'ab • Religion • August 25, 2026

1. What is Maulid An-Nabiyy?

Maulid An-Nabiyy refers to the celebration of the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ birthday, commonly associated with 12th Rabi' al-Awwal.

Such gatherings may involve recitation of poems about the Prophet ﷺ, singing, feeding people and, in some places, drumming or other activities.

The intention behind these gatherings is often to express love for the Prophet ﷺ. However, love for the Prophet should be expressed according to the guidance of Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.

2. Was Maulid Celebrated by the Prophet ﷺ and the Sahabah?

One of the central questions concerning Maulid is whether the Prophet ﷺ himself, his Companions, or the earliest generations of Muslims celebrated his birthday as a religious occasion.

The Prophet ﷺ did not establish an annual birthday celebration.

During the 23 years of his prophethood, there is no established report that he gathered his Companions annually to celebrate his birth.

The Companions were among the people who loved the Prophet ﷺ most. Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali and the other Companions followed his guidance closely.

The argument presented by this view is that if an annual religious celebration of his birth had been prescribed, the earliest generations would have been among the first to practise it.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

"Whoever introduces into this matter of ours that which is not from it, it is rejected."

— Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim

3. Why Say No?

a. The Concern About Religious Innovation

Those who reject the celebration of Maulid argue that acts of worship require evidence from the Qur'an and Sunnah.

Allah says:

"This day I have perfected for you your religion."

— Qur'an 5:3

From this perspective, introducing a new annual religious celebration is considered an addition to religious practice that was not established by the Prophet ﷺ or his Companions.

b. Avoiding Exaggeration

Another concern raised by scholars who oppose Maulid is that some forms of celebration may contain excessive praise or practices that go beyond what is established in the Sunnah.

The Prophet ﷺ warned:

"Do not exaggerate about me as the Christians exaggerated about the son of Maryam."

— Sahih al-Bukhari

c. Uncertainty About the Exact Birth Date

Historians and scholars have differed regarding the exact date on which the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was born.

5. What Should We Do Instead?

If we want to honour and love the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, there are many established ways to do so throughout the year.

  1. Learn his Sunnah and make an effort to live according to his teachings.
  2. Send abundant Salawat upon the Prophet ﷺ, such as: Allahumma salli 'ala Muhammad.
  3. Fast on Mondays. The Prophet ﷺ mentioned Monday in connection with the day on which he was born and the day revelation came to him.
  4. Teach his Seerah to our children and help them understand his character, teachings and mission.
  5. Call to Tawheed and uphold the central message of Islam that the Prophet ﷺ brought.

The Prophet ﷺ was asked about fasting on Monday and said:

"That is the day I was born and the day revelation came to me."

— Sahih Muslim

Conclusion

Saying "No to Maulid An-Nabiyy" is not saying "No to the Prophet ﷺ." Rather, those who hold this position see it as saying yes to following the Sunnah and avoiding religious innovations.

The safest path, according to this understanding, is to follow the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ and the example of his Companions.

Love for the Prophet ﷺ should be shown through obedience, learning, good character and following his guidance.

Allah says:

"And whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him and follows other than the way of the believers — We will give him what he has taken and drive him into Hell."

— Qur'an 4:115

May Allah grant us sincerity, beneficial knowledge and the ability to follow the Sunnah. Ameen.
